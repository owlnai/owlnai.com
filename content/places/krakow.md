I spent four days in Kraków in June 2026, meeting some online friends for the first time. Each of us came from a different country, and we picked Kraków as the place to meet in the middle.

Even though I had seen them hundreds of times, it was always through a screen. When I got to the airport, they were waiting for me. I couldn't find them, but they were looking at me wondering when I'd notice. After giving up, they gave me a sign. I did recognize them before but I wasn't totally sure and I wanted to avoid embarrassment. 

What stayed with me were the evenings. In June the sun barely wants to set, and the banks of the Vistula fill up with people sitting on the grass, boats moored along the quay, nobody in any hurry to go home. I'd brought my camera, and I spent most evenings walking along the river chasing sunsets until the sky turned orange, with a swan keeping me company.

![Sunset over the river, the sky streaked orange behind a dark line of trees and buildings, reflected on the water beside a stone embankment.](/places/krakow/river-sunset.jpg)
![A white swan gliding across rippling water that reflects warm evening light.](/places/krakow/swan.jpg)

The city was full of restaurants and shops. We were all sharing a small loft, and right in front of us was every kind of restaurant you could think of: Italian, Indian, Mexican, Japanese, traditional Polish and even some American chains. I ordered some burgers and I still miss them, haha.

I also noticed a Żabka, the 24/7 convenience store, every few blocks. And even though Poland is in the EU, it has its own currency, the złoty. You can pay by card almost everywhere, so there's no need to carry cash, but card machines often offer to charge you in your home currency instead. Always pick złoty: the conversion they give you is expensive.

By day, Wawel's old brick clock tower stood against a sky so blue it looked painted.

![The brick clock tower of Wawel Cathedral, with its ornate clock face and green copper top, against a deep blue sky.](/places/krakow/wawel-clock-tower.jpg)

The tower belongs to Wawel, a big castle in the middle of the city, and around it were impressive statues and a crowded street market.

A few months after I got home, I saw in the news that a horrible attack had taken place at the town hall, in the same old town I'd been walking through. I couldn't believe I'd been on those streets not so long before. I pray for the victims' souls.

Two of my friends were Polish, although from different cities. They spoke to me about the castle towers in their towns, and how good Gdańsk is. I'd love to visit it one day.
