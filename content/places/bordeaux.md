I spent a week of my 2025 vacation in Bordeaux.

I still remember arriving and feeling the warmth that made me fall for the city: the way people treated each other, the busy streets lined with shops, the beautiful French architecture, everything I could need just around the corner. I've always lived in a small town, almost a village, and Bordeaux felt like a second home right away.

I spent those days living and coworking near the center, and what stayed with me most were the people. The florist next to where I was staying, who offered me water because they could tell I wasn't from around there and the sun was beating down that day. The kind person at the hardware shop on the corner, who helped me work out the strange way my apartment door opened. The woman at McDonald's who asked how my stay was going and, noticing my French wasn't fluent, gave me a few tips.

And the habit, or simply the good manners, of saying "Bonjour" and "Merci, au revoir" every time we got on the bus, when back home you're lucky if people even look the driver in the eye.

Little things like that made me stop and think. What we're used to, what we take as normal, is done differently somewhere else, and there's so much we can learn from that.

The food and the places were spectacular, of course. I won't lie... I left wanting to come back!
