I went to Liège in October 2025, a few days before Halloween, and stayed for four or five days. The first thing to greet me, in the hall at Brussels Airport, was Tintin's rocket.

I reached Liège after dark and got off at Guillemins station. Downstairs, a few homeless people had set up camp to shelter from the rain. The guards kept chasing them away, even though they weren't bothering anyone. Once I'd left my things at the hotel, I went back and talked with some of them about life and how they'd ended up there. People often treat them as outcasts, but it could be anyone you know, even your friend, if life dealt them a bad hand.

The days that followed were grey and cold, the kind of autumn when the parks are covered in fallen leaves and the whole city feels a little quieter. I visited a few museums and restaurants, but the place I keep coming back to is the park in front of La Boverie: quiet, with a small lake full of ducks and benches made for passing the morning. It felt like an oasis in the desert: a bubble away from the rumble of the city.

![A path covered in fallen plane-tree leaves beside a pond, under bare trees and an overcast sky.](/places/liege/park-leaves.jpg)

The residential neighbourhoods were lovely too. On an early morning walk, people out on their runs greeted me as if I lived there. Narrow houses with old European façades, tall thin blocks of apartments, and a grey sky that every now and then let through a moment of sunlight.

What surprised me most was how good everyone's English was. I bought a phone charger at the local MediaMarkt and the clerk switched straight into a perfect British accent. You need some French to get by, but English will take you a long way.

Belgium speaks multiple languages, so they're used to it. In fact, I almost got lost because the train sign from Leuven to Liège said "Luik" (Dutch!) and I thought it was a different town!

In some ways Liège felt more European than other cities I've been to. There were far more foreigners and tourists than I expected, and I could find a Spanish tourist at every tram stop. It can also feel unsafe at night, so be careful if you're travelling alone.

I stayed at YUST, and I'd recommend it to anyone visiting Liège or Antwerp. The deluxe room is fairly cheap for a European city, and it felt like the best parts of a coworking space, an apartment and a hotel, without the downsides. It changed what I expect from a hotel, to be honest.

It was different from [Bordeaux](/places/bordeaux), but it had its own magic. Familiar, yet cold. I'd go back, this time with more time. Can't believe I missed the cat café and the stairs of the Montagne de Bueren!
